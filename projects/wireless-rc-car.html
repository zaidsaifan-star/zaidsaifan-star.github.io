Wireless RC Car with Electronic Differential

A custom RC car driven from a PS5 controller. Each rear wheel has its own motor, and the firmware sets their speeds from the steering angle so the car corners without the inside wheel slipping.

My roleSteering, chassis, electronic differential
  • Arduino C++
  • Python
  • SolidWorks
  • L298N
  • PWM

ProblemWhy an electronic differential

A fixed rear axle forces both wheels to turn at the same speed. In a corner the inside wheel travels a shorter arc, so one wheel has to slip, which shows up as understeer and lost traction.

Giving each rear wheel its own motor lets the firmware set a speed offset from the steering input, so each wheel turns at the speed the corner asks for.

FirmwareControl path

  • A PS5 controller pairs to a Python interface that turns stick positions into throttle and steering commands.
  • Commands go wirelessly to C++ firmware on the Arduino, which handles PWM timing and the differential math.
  • The loop is short, so the car responds without noticeable lag.

ElectricalPower and motor hardware

  • An 11.1 V 3S LiPo powers the whole car, split by dual buck converters so logic and actuators never share a rail.
  • An L298N dual H-bridge drives both rear motors.
  • The steering servo has its own supply, so a stall cannot brown out the microcontroller mid-corner.
  • I designed the circuit and wiring layout and selected every part against a BOM.

MechanicalChassis

I modelled the chassis in SolidWorks to package the steering servo, motors and electronics, then completed the mechanical assembly. The car was demonstrated live to club leadership, which tested control response, chassis integrity and firmware stability under real driving.
