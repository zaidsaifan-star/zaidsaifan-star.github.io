Autonomous Golf Cart: Power Distribution & Throttle Control

A student team is turning a full-scale golf cart into an autonomous vehicle. I work on throttle and motor control, and co-designed the board that carries motor current and hands out clean logic rails.

My roleThrottle & motor control; power distribution board co-design
  • Altium Designer
  • PWM
  • ESC
  • IPC-2221
  • 24 V LiFePO₄

ContextThe brief

Everything downstream of the cart's 24 V traction pack, the motor drive, a single-board computer, four microcontrollers and their sensors, was being wired point to point. On a vehicle that drives itself, loose high-current wiring is a safety problem first.

The fix was one board that carries motor-level current and supplies clean rails to the compute stack.

My subsystemThrottle control

  • Programmed a microcontroller to generate PWM throttle commands for a high-current ESC, automating acceleration on the full-scale cart.
  • Wired, bench-tested and debugged the system for repeatable speed regulation before it went on the vehicle.

Three tiersPower architecture

  • The 24 V rail runs straight to the motor driver through its own fuse and bulk capacitance, keeping the highest-current path short.
  • A 24 → 12 V buck feeds everything that is not the drive motor.
  • A 12 → 5 V stage supplies a Raspberry Pi 5 and four ESP32 controllers.
  • Voltage, current and thermal margin were checked on every rail, not just the input.

SchematicOn the board

  • Battery Connect brings the 24 V pack onto the board through a screw-terminal block.
  • Fuse 1 protects the branch that feeds the 24 → 12 V buck converter for the logic tier.
  • Fuse 2 protects the motor branch, which passes a 1000 µF bulk capacitor (C1) before the Pololu motor driver output.
  • All grounds return on one shared net, and every connector uses the same screw-terminal footprint, so the harness is simple to service.

Copper sizingCarrying the current

  • Trace widths calculated to IPC-2221 for a 15–20 A continuous target.
  • 2 oz copper on both layers, with polygon pours instead of discrete traces.
  • Via stitching so both pours share the load and spread the heat.

Heat dissipation was a placement constraint from the first layout pass.

SafetyProtection

A 5 A fuse on the logic branch and a 20 A fuse on the motor branch, coordinated with the driver's current limit so the fuse acts last. Bulk capacitance holds the motor rail up through current steps, and anti-spark connectors protect the contacts when the 100 Ah pack is connected. The board shipped with a full BOM and wiring documentation for design review.
